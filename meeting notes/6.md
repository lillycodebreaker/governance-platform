Meeting Summary — AI Governance Platform Consolidation, Migration, Production Readiness & Strategic Roadmap

Meeting focus: Review the consolidation of existing AI registration and inventory platforms, discuss data migration and production readiness, establish operational support and reporting requirements, and align on the long-term vision for the AI Governance engineering team.

Overall takeaway: The new AI Governance platform has consolidated functionality previously distributed across two systems. The immediate priorities are completing data migration, managing the transition from legacy systems, preparing production support, and improving governance reporting. Leadership also outlined a broader vision for expanding the platform into additional governance and compliance capabilities.

Note: The transcript has some speech-recognition errors. The summary distinguishes confirmed decisions, current implementation status, and future recommendations.

1. Key Discussion Notes

A. Current State — Two Disconnected Platforms

Previously, AI model use case registration and inventory management were supported by two separate applications.

Component	Function	Challenges
AI Platform	Model use case registration and initial BigQuery storage	Limited customization and slow response to governance change requests
AI Model Inventory	Governance-facing inventory, dashboard, and APIs	Requires separate data synchronization, cleanup, and maintenance
Data pipeline	Copies and transforms AI Platform records into another BigQuery dataset	Duplicate data, additional complexity, and maintenance burden

Additional challenges:

* Two engineering teams maintain overlapping capabilities.
* Governance users lack direct control over the registration experience.
* The AI Platform has technical limitations on required registration fields.
* Data must be copied and transformed before governance teams can use it.
* Registration, inventory, and review experiences are disconnected.

Business objective: Consolidate registration, inventory, review, and reporting into one governance-controlled platform.

The new platform provides:

Current status: Core capabilities are already deployed to production and undergoing beta testing. Historical data migration is still in progress.

C. Data Migration and Legacy System Transition

The engineering team described a migration approach that supports repeated synchronization.

* Historical AI Platform records will be migrated into MongoDB.
* Migration can be triggered multiple times to capture additional legacy records.
* The migration design should prevent duplicate records.
* Existing records modified in the new platform should not be overwritten by subsequent migration runs.

Target timing discussed: The team anticipated migration and broader transition readiness within approximately two weeks, with the end of November described as the wider target.

These were estimates rather than formal commitments.

Key concern — Temporary coexistence of two platforms

Leadership raised two scenarios:

* A user continues creating registrations in the legacy AI Platform after the new platform launches.
* A user registers or updates information in the new platform, but legacy reports continue using the old dataset.

The first scenario can potentially be addressed through recurring synchronization.

The second is more complicated because the new platform may not have write access to the legacy BigQuery environment.

Risk: Registration totals and management reports may become inconsistent if different stakeholders continue relying on different data sources.

The team discussed making the new platform the authoritative source while coordinating the transition and legacy reporting dependencies.

D. Production Readiness and Deployment Strategy

Several operational questions were raised:

* Is rollback capability available?
* Should blue-green deployment be supported?
* What happens if the new platform becomes unavailable?
* Can the legacy platform temporarily serve as a fallback?

Leadership clarified that:

* Blue-green deployment is not currently implemented.
* The application is not considered sufficiently critical at this stage to justify additional deployment complexity.
* Short outages may be acceptable under the current operating model.
* More advanced availability strategies can be revisited as the platform becomes more business-critical.

Key direction: Avoid overengineering the initial release, but establish appropriate operational readiness.

E. Migration Communication and Leadership Approval

Navdeep emphasized that the platform transition requires more than technical readiness.

Areas requiring coordination include:

* Communicating the new registration location to users.
* Updating or redirecting links on the legacy AI Platform.
* Aligning with existing platform owners.
* Identifying management reports that still use legacy data.
* Ensuring the appropriate leaders agree on the transition.

Navdeep specifically requested that the team check with him before the production cutover.

He also indicated that he would investigate reporting dependencies and coordinate the necessary leadership conversations.

F. Operational Support and ServiceNow Tickets

With the platform moving toward broader production use, leadership highlighted the need for a formal support model.

The concern came from experience with other products where:

* Users opened ServiceNow tickets against application IDs.
* Engineering teams had limited visibility into incoming tickets.
* Unaddressed tickets were escalated to senior leadership.
* Support processes had not kept pace with platform adoption.

The AI Governance team should proactively establish:

* A support rotation.
* Ownership of ServiceNow tickets.
* Ticket routing and notification mechanisms.
* Application-level monitoring.
* Incident escalation procedures.
* Clear operational responsibilities.

A support-ticket notification tool was mentioned, but its exact name is unclear in the transcript.

G. Auditability, Traceability, and Success Metrics

Engineering leadership raised two important improvement areas beyond basic registration functionality.

Auditability and traceability

* Record governance decisions and supporting rationale.
* Track review comments and approvals.
* Maintain relevant documentation and workflow history.
* Provide evidence of how assessments and decisions were made.

Operational performance metrics

The platform should automatically calculate information currently collected manually by the Governance team.

Potential metrics include:

Leadership noted that Crystal already reports some of these metrics manually.

Goal: Build dashboards that automatically provide the information needed by Governance and leadership.

H. Long-Term Platform Vision

Navdeep described a broader ambition for the AI Governance platform.

Near-term priority: Fully support AI Governance and satisfy Crystal’s team’s operational requirements.

Long-term opportunity: Expand the underlying platform into additional enterprise governance and compliance domains.

Supporting principles:

* Build reusable platform capabilities.
* Integrate with systems where AI assets are created and deployed.
* Automate information collection wherever possible.
* Establish a centralized source of governance information.
* Reuse the existing enterprise technology stack.
* Avoid unnecessary duplication of infrastructure and frameworks.

The discussion explicitly indicated that this broader expansion vision should remain internal for now.

6. Key Takeaways for Leadership

1. The platform consolidation is a major milestone. Functionality previously split across registration and inventory systems is now available through a single application.

2. Data migration and reporting alignment are the most important cutover dependencies. Technical migration alone is insufficient if management continues receiving inconsistent numbers from legacy systems.

3. Production readiness must include operational support. Formal ticket monitoring, support rotations, and escalation procedures should be established before adoption expands.

4. Auditability and automated reporting are important next investments. These capabilities can reduce Governance’s manual reporting effort and demonstrate the platform’s business impact.

5. Avoid unnecessary infrastructure complexity. Advanced deployment strategies can be introduced as business criticality increases, while maintaining appropriate recovery and support procedures now.

6. The platform has a broader strategic opportunity. Once AI Governance is stable, its reusable workflow, registration, and compliance capabilities could support additional enterprise governance domains.

7. Recommended Immediate Leadership Focus

For the next engineering and leadership review, I would prioritize:

* Migration and cutover readiness: Confirm data completeness, synchronization strategy, legacy report dependencies, and Navdeep’s approval requirements.
* Production operational readiness: Assign support responsibilities, verify ServiceNow ticket visibility, and establish incident response procedures.
* Governance measurement framework: Partner with Crystal to identify the highest-value metrics and automate existing manual reports.

Overall recommendation: Complete the migration and establish operational stability first, then invest in auditability, measurable business outcomes, and broader integration. This gives the AI Governance platform a stable foundation for its longer-term enterprise governance vision.
