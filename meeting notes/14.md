E. AI-Assisted Risk Assessment and Control Assignment

The discussion referenced an existing reviewer agent that helps analyze registered use cases.

The agent can support:

* Retrieving use case information.
* Recommending risk categories.
* Assessing risk levels.
* Identifying areas of impact.
* Recommending applicable controls.
* Allowing reviewers to verify or adjust recommendations.
* Updating governance records through available integrations.

Important principle: AI should support governance decisions, but human reviewers remain responsible for final judgments.

The discussion also raised the importance of handling low-confidence agent responses and escalating uncertain outcomes for human review.

F. Human-in-the-Loop Automation

The broader goal is to automate routine processing while retaining human involvement when necessary.

Potential applications include:

* Automatically prepopulating registration information.
* Identifying missing information.
* Recommending controls based on policies.
* Validating control responses.
* Routing cases to the appropriate reviewers.
* Tracking approvals and outstanding actions.
* Maintaining decision history and supporting evidence.

The target is not merely to automate form completion, but to support an end-to-end governance lifecycle.

G. Production Deployment and Governance Controls

The conversation touched on connecting governance requirements with application and model deployment processes.

A future workflow could:

* Detect when an AI use case or model approaches production.
* Verify required registration and governance reviews.
* Confirm applicable controls and approvals.
* Identify unresolved compliance requirements.
* Provide a deployment readiness result to the relevant platform.

This was described as a strategic automation opportunity rather than a fully implemented control.

H. Model Registration and November Delivery

Model use case registration was discussed as the immediate engineering priority.

The new platform aims to replace legacy functionality with a more consistent registration experience.

Capabilities mentioned include:

* Multi-step forms.
* Draft saving.
* Required-field validation.
* Registration duplication.
* Inventory browsing and management.
* Reviewer comments.
* Audit history.
* Integration with existing data sources.

Target milestone: Complete the model registration modernization and transition by the end of November.

The discussion also acknowledged that production deployment requires business validation, technical testing, and communication with users of the legacy system.

I. Quickbase Migration Strategy

Quickbase currently supports significant GenAI Governance functionality.

The team is planning migration in stages.

Phase	Work
Frontend modernization	Move the GenAI registration and review UI into the new AI Governance portal
Transitional backend	Continue using Quickbase data and workflows where necessary
Backend modernization	Replace Quickbase-specific logic, automation, and storage
Full integration	Consolidate governance records and support new automation capabilities

Key principle: Users should experience a consistent portal regardless of which backend system is still supporting the workflow.

J. Third-Party AI Automation

Paul Marie’s third-party AI governance process was also discussed.

Today, Archer IRQ records and AI Governance registration may require separate actions.

The modernization effort aims to:

* Identify relevant AI use cases from Archer submissions.
* Reuse existing request information.

* Notify business owners to review and complete missing fields.
* Track outstanding requests and reminders.
* Reduce repetitive manual follow-ups.

6. Key Takeaways for Leadership

1. Centralization remains the core platform strategy. AI registration, risk review, inventory, controls, and approvals should move toward one integrated experience.

2. Engineering should prioritize delivery before broader expansion. Model registration and related November milestones are the immediate commitments.

3. Automation should extend beyond form completion. The long-term opportunity includes risk classification, control assignment, evidence review, approval routing, and deployment integration.

4. Human accountability remains essential. Automated recommendations must support reviewer oversight, traceability, and appropriate escalation.

5. Architecture alignment and engineering ownership are critical. Clear designs, direct cross-functional collaboration, and disciplined execution will support scalability.

6. The new engineering leader should establish structure without slowing delivery. The team needs clear ownership, a prioritized roadmap, and consistent requirements documentation while continuing existing implementation work.
