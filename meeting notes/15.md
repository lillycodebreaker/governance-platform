1. Executive Summary

Crystal outlined her vision for AI Governance modernization and the partnership between Governance and Engineering.

The main goal is to move from manual, reactive AI Governance to an automated, proactive, risk-based process that identifies and reviews AI use cases before production deployment.

The discussion reinforced that Engineering should help shape both the near-term delivery plan and the longer-term strategy.

3. Crystal’s Main Priorities

A. Fix the model inventory first

Today, Governance relies heavily on model owners to voluntarily register models.

Problems include:

* Incomplete or delayed registrations.
* Manual information collection.
* Limited visibility into production AI models.
* Difficulty tracking compliance across the lifecycle.

Target: Capture relevant information earlier and establish a reliable enterprise AI inventory.

B. Connect the entire AI lifecycle

The proposed lifecycle is:

Use Case Ideation & Intake

Risk Assessment & Control Assignment

Cross-Functional Reviews

Approval & Control Implementation

Model Registration & Deployment

Ongoing Monitoring & Governance

Target-state lifecycle, not a fully implemented workflow today.

Information collected during intake should be reused during later model registration, risk reviews, and monitoring instead of requiring repeated data entry.

C. Make governance risk-based

Not all AI use cases require the same level of review.

* Lower-risk internal use cases may qualify for streamlined reviews.
* Higher-risk cases, particularly clinical or patient-facing AI, may require additional Privacy, Security, Patient Safety, and Architecture assessments.
* AI Governance owns responsible-use risk requirements, not every enterprise approval function.

Engineering opportunity: Automate routing based on approved policies and risk criteria.

D. Introduce proactive governance

Crystal’s desired future state is to know whether an AI use case has met its governance requirements before it enters production.

This requires integrating Governance with development and deployment environments.

The future platform could share approval status with the deployment system and flag missing controls.

This is a strategic goal, not an existing production gate.

8. Key Leadership Takeaways

Crystal’s immediate priority is operational effectiveness, not adding more features. She wants the existing manual governance processes modernized, starting with inventory completeness and lifecycle integration.

Auditability matters, but capacity is constrained. The platform should be designed to support audit trails from the start, even if more advanced capabilities are delivered incrementally.

The biggest long-term opportunity is enterprise integration. Automated discovery, risk-based reviews, and deployment readiness checks could significantly strengthen governance coverage.

The Engineering–Governance partnership needs a consistent operating model. Regular alignment on policies, priorities, workflows, and acceptance criteria will reduce rework.

9. What I Should Focus on as Lead Director

My three most important next steps are:

* Learn the business: Understand Crystal’s policies, review processes, controls, and operational challenges.
* Deliver the foundation: Keep the engineering team focused on model inventory and GenAI intake modernization.
* Build the automation roadmap: Identify where AI agents, workflow automation, integrations, and auditability can provide the highest value.

Bottom line: My responsibility is to turn Crystal’s governance requirements into a scalable, automated, auditable platform, while balancing immediate engineering commitments with the longer-term goal of proactive enterprise AI Governance.
