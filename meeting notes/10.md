Evolution

* Platform integrations.
* AI-enabled capabilities.

Strategic direction: Make enterprise analytical capabilities easier to discover, access, and use through a centralized platform experience.

D. Enterprise Data Movement and the Platform Strategy

Navdeep spent time explaining EDM because its evolution provides a useful model for AI Governance.

EDM originally focused on standardizing how data entered the enterprise analytical environment.

The platform now supports:

* Data ingestion into the bronze layer.
* Data movement out of the enterprise platform.
* Batch and streaming processing.
* Future change data capture capabilities.
* Transformation across bronze, silver, and gold layers.

An updated EDM capability, referred to internally as EDM 3, is intended to improve usability, efficiency, and transformation support.

Navdeep indicated the broader environment supports approximately 60,000 jobs and moves petabytes of data weekly.

Key strategic lesson: EDM initially gained adoption through established platform requirements. Future growth depends increasingly on offering a better, lower-friction experience than competing tools.

He wants the AI Governance platform to follow a similar path.

E. Long-Term Enterprise Data Architecture

Navdeep described the enterprise vision of making analytical data discoverable and accessible across systems.

The preferred primary analytical environment is BigQuery / GCP, although other platforms remain in use, including Databricks, Snowflake, and additional enterprise technologies.

Long-term goals include:

* Cataloging analytical data across platforms.
* Reducing unnecessary duplication.
* Supporting linked datasets and zero-copy approaches where practical.
* Encouraging adoption through ease of use and cost efficiency.
* Providing reusable enterprise data products and services.

Relevance to AI Governance: The governance platform should similarly integrate with existing enterprise systems instead of expecting every business unit to abandon its current technology immediately.

2. AI Governance Platform — Strategic Vision

A. First Priority: Consolidate Existing Governance Capabilities

Historically, the AI Governance team relied on borrowed engineering resources and separate utilities built across different organizations.

The newly funded engineering team is responsible for consolidating those capabilities into a cohesive platform.

Near-term objectives:

* Consolidate registration and inventory capabilities.
* Modernize existing AI Governance workflows.
* Improve user experience.
* Automate repetitive activities.
* Establish dependable engineering ownership.
* Meet Crystal’s team’s operational requirements.

Navdeep made clear that the immediate priority is to make AI Governance successful before expanding into additional areas.

B. Future Vision: Expand Governance Platform Services

Once the AI Governance platform is mature, Navdeep wants to explore offering reusable capabilities to other governance and compliance functions.

AI Governance Platform

Registration · Inventory · Risk review · Controls · Approvals

Reusable Governance Services

Workflow automation · Evidence tracking · Decision history · Notifications · APIs

Information Security

Audit

Data Governance

Provisioning

The proposed expansion would focus on offering technical services to these functions, not taking ownership of their governance responsibilities.

Navdeep specifically emphasized that the team would not own Audit, Information Security, or infrastructure provisioning.

This broader platform expansion was described as a long-term internal vision, not a formally approved cross-enterprise program.

C. Proactive AI Use Case Discovery and Governance

A major challenge is identifying AI use cases created across CVS.

Different business units can provision applications through multiple environments and platforms.

The target approach is to:

* Identify where new AI-related applications and models are created.
* Integrate with relevant provisioning and development systems.
* Detect which use cases require AI Governance.
* Automatically initiate applicable reviews.
* Avoid unnecessary governance friction for out-of-scope use cases.
* Reduce the likelihood that AI systems enter production without required oversight.

Desired outcome: Bring AI assets into governance early without forcing users to complete unnecessary manual steps.

D. Integrate Governance with Enterprise Provisioning

Navdeep described how a future integrated workflow could operate.

1. New AI Use Case

Business team proposes an AI solution

2. Governance Evaluation

Risk classification, required controls, and relevant approvals

3. Approval Information Shared

Governance records and applicable approvals passed to provisioning systems

4. Infrastructure Provisioning

Prime or another platform uses relevant approval status without repeated data entry

Key opportunity: Reuse governance approvals and relevant registration information across systems to reduce duplicated effort and accelerate delivery.

8. Key Takeaways for Leadership

1. The immediate mandate is execution. Consolidate fragmented AI Governance capabilities and deliver a dependable platform that meets Crystal’s team’s requirements.

2. The long-term opportunity is broader than AI Governance. The architecture should be reusable across other governance and compliance functions without taking over their business ownership.

3. Enterprise integration is critical. The platform should identify relevant AI use cases early and exchange information with provisioning, architecture, and other enterprise systems.

4. User experience drives adoption. As with EDM and Data Portal, the long-term goal is to create a platform that teams choose because it reduces friction and improves efficiency.

5. Team formation is an immediate leadership responsibility. Finalizing reporting relationships, hiring, engineering ownership, and collaboration practices will directly affect delivery success.

6. Regular leadership alignment matters. Navdeep wants structured communication and early escalation of issues without unnecessary administrative overhead.
