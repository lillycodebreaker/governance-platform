Meeting Summary — AI Governance Platform: GenAI Use Case Migration, Workflow Design & User Experience

Meeting focus: Align AI Governance and Engineering on migrating GenAI use case registration from Quickbase to the new centralized platform, agree on the phased migration approach, and identify improvements to registration, risk review, controls, documentation, and AI-assisted workflows.

Overall takeaway: The team agreed to migrate the GenAI registration user interface first while retaining Quickbase as the backend, then gradually replace the remaining backend automation and reporting dependencies. The new platform will provide a unified experience for requesters and reviewers while improving visibility, auditability, and automation.

Note: Some names and technical terms are unclear in the transcript. The summary distinguishes agreed directions from future enhancements and open design questions.

1. Key Discussion Notes

A. Current Progress and November Milestone

The engineering team has already consolidated two legacy model registration capabilities into the new AI Governance platform:

* Model use case registration previously supported through the AI Platform.
* Model inventory functionality developed as a separate application.
* Historical model data migration remains in progress.

Target: Complete the model registration transition by the end of November, enabling the new platform to serve as the primary destination for model use case registration.

Once that foundation is stable, the next priority is migrating GenAI use case intake from Quickbase.

B. Quickbase Migration — Three Major Workstreams

Engineering identified three primary components that must eventually move from Quickbase.

Workstream	Scope	Proposed approach
User experience	Customer intake, reviewer workflows, risk assessment, controls	Migrate first
Backend workflows	Automated routing, status management, notifications, business logic	Retain temporarily; migrate later
Data and reporting	Quickbase records, Composer jobs, BigQuery, downstream reporting	Migrate in later phases

Key decision: Separate frontend modernization from backend replacement so users can benefit from the improved experience sooner.

Phase 1 — New Governance UI

GenAI intake · Reviewer workflow · Risk reports · Registration status

Temporary Quickbase Backend

Existing business rules · Workflow automation · Notifications · Data

Phase 2 — Backend Modernization

Replace Quickbase workflows and data dependencies while preserving the user experience

Timeline discussed: Begin GenAI UI migration after the November model registration milestone. Engineering estimated approximately one month of implementation work, subject to requirements, staffing, testing, and business acceptance.

No firm GenAI production date was approved.

C. GenAI Registration User Experience

The team reviewed features already available in the model registration interface that could be reused for GenAI intake.

* Multi-step registration forms.
* Automatic draft saving, currently approximately every two minutes.
* Required-field validation.
* Draft editing and continuation.
* Draft deletion.
* Registration duplication.
* AI Assistant placeholder for contextual guidance.
* Registration lists with status information.

Business stakeholders agreed that these provide a useful starting point.

Approach: Reuse the existing design framework and refine GenAI-specific requirements with Almira, Stephanie, Crystal, and the relevant governance reviewers.

D. Editing Approved Use Cases

The discussion highlighted an important difference between editing a draft and changing an already-approved AI use case.

Proposed rules:

Change type	Proposed handling
Draft registration	Requester can edit freely
Minor ownership/contact updates	Potentially allow limited direct editing
Changes to approved data use or scope	Require governance review
Material expansion of functionality	Submit a change request linked to the original use case
Reviewer updates	Allow authorized editing with audit history

The team agreed that users should not be able to change material approved details without Governance review.

Important future capability: Introduce a structured change-request workflow rather than requiring users to create unrelated new registrations.

E. POC-to-Production Lifecycle Tracking

The existing process may create multiple registrations for the same AI product when moving from proof of concept to production.

The team discussed improving this experience.

* Preserve the relationship between the original POC and production deployment.
* Require users to confirm or update information that has changed.
* Capture differences between test/mock data and actual production data.
* Avoid blindly duplicating a POC registration.
* Track the transition without losing review history.

Open design question: Should production require a new linked registration, an extension of the existing record, or another lifecycle action?

No final decision was made.

F. Reviewer Inventory and Case Management

The new model inventory interface was demonstrated as an alternative to the existing Quickbase Kanban-style board.

The proposed GenAI reviewer experience includes:

* Searchable inventory list.
* Configurable columns.
* Filtering and sorting.
* CSV export.
* Reviewer comments.
* Record history.
* Role-based editing.
* Soft deletion and controlled permanent deletion.

The Governance team clarified that the Quickbase Kanban layout is not a requirement.

Agreed direction: Use a searchable, filterable inventory rather than replicating the existing Kanban board.

The transcript indicated approximately 7,000 GenAI records may need to be supported.

Engineering noted that server-side pagination would be appropriate for this volume.

G. Separate Requester and Reviewer Status Views

The team identified different information needs for requesters and reviewers.

User group	Information needed
Business requester	Submitted, in review, additional information required, approved, or other high-level status
Governance reviewer	Detailed routing, review categories, assigned controls, risk disposition, and workflow steps

Agreed direction: Provide simpler, understandable status information to requesters while preserving detailed operational states for reviewers.

Approved, inactive, or abandoned records do not need to dominate the default review queue but must remain searchable.

H. Risk Reports and Control Acknowledgment

The Governance team emphasized that the risk report is a critical artifact.

A requester should understand:

* Why a use case was approved.
* Which controls apply.
* Whether approval is conditional.
* What actions are required before deployment.

Key concern: Some users see the approval status and assume their obligations are complete, even when controls remain outstanding.

The new platform should make risk reports readily accessible and clearly communicate approval conditions.

Proposed enhancements include:

* A risk report section within the registration.
* An accessible risk report action from the registration list.
* Explicit visibility of assigned controls.
* Potential acknowledgment of approval conditions.
* Tracking control implementation responses.

I. Control Implementation and Follow-up Automation

Today, Governance often collects control implementation details through manual emails.

The team discussed moving this responsibility into the portal.

Target workflow:

* Governance assigns applicable risk controls.
* The requester views the assigned controls.
* The requester documents how each control is implemented.
* Governance reviews the information.
* Responses, decisions, and supporting evidence are stored with the use case.

This aligns with the separate control-validation agent initiative, where AI could eventually help identify incomplete control responses before manual review.

J. Reviewer Notes, Evidence, and Auditability

The team discussed preserving several types of information within each use case.

* Governance reviewer notes.
* Patient Safety review notes.
* Risk assessment outcomes.
* Supporting documents.
* Meeting notes or transcripts.
* Review comments and decisions.
* Control implementation evidence.

Proposed direction: Support a centralized record of governance-related artifacts.

The team discussed optional supporting-document uploads during registration and potentially allowing authorized reviewers to add documentation later.

Some reviewer notes may remain internal, while others could be visible to requesters. Exact visibility rules need further definition.

K. Customer-Facing and Reviewer AI Assistants

The team discussed two AI Assistant experiences.

Customer-facing assistant

* Explain registration questions.
* Provide field-level guidance and FAQs.
* Draw on existing Quickbase user guides and preparation materials.
* Help users understand the intake and review process.

Reviewer-facing assistant

* Retrieve information from use cases and attached artifacts.
* Help reviewers answer questions about use cases, risks, and controls.
* Recommend applicable controls.
* Provide references to supporting source documents.
* Support portfolio-level queries.

An example discussed was allowing Privacy reviewers to ask how PHI is used by a particular AI use case and retrieve relevant supporting evidence.

These capabilities were presented as potential extensions of existing agent work, not necessarily requirements for the first GenAI UI release.

L. Landing Page, User Guidance, and Announcements

A new requirement emerged around improving the Governance platform landing page.

Potential capabilities include:

* Overview of what AI Governance does.
* Guidance on when registration is required.
* Visual explanations of the review workflow.
* Expected steps and turnaround information.
* FAQs and user guides.
* Tutorials or AI-generated walkthrough videos.
* Announcements about known issues.
* Links to appropriate support teams or alternative resources.
* Guidance on available sandbox environments.

The goal is to reduce confusion and help users determine the correct next step before creating a registration.

M. Downstream System Dependencies

Crystal raised an important migration risk.

Several downstream systems currently consume Quickbase data or APIs.

Changes to field names, schema, or data access could disrupt those integrations.

Required before cutover: Identify impacted downstream consumers, communicate planned changes, and coordinate integration updates.

5. Suggested Implementation Phases

Phase 1 — Unified GenAI User Experience

Migrate customer intake and reviewer functionality into the Governance platform, reusing existing registration components while retaining Quickbase backend workflows.

Phase 2 — Enhanced Lifecycle and Review Management

Add controlled editing, POC-to-production linkage, control acknowledgment, implementation evidence, reviewer notes, and improved dashboards.

Phase 3 — Full Automation and Quickbase Retirement

Migrate backend automation and reporting, expand AI Assistant capabilities, automate control validation, and coordinate downstream integration changes.

These are suggested phases, not formal delivery commitments.

6. Key Takeaways for Leadership

1. A unified user experience is achievable before full Quickbase retirement. Migrating the frontend first reduces immediate complexity while allowing users to benefit from a modern interface.

2. GenAI migration is an opportunity to improve governance processes. The work should not simply reproduce Quickbase limitations, particularly around editing, lifecycle tracking, and risk-control acknowledgment.

3. POC-to-production continuity is a major improvement opportunity. The platform should preserve the relationship between early experiments and production use cases while requiring updated information and review.

4. Governance decisions need to be actionable. An approval status alone is insufficient when additional controls must be implemented. Requesters need clear visibility into their obligations.

5. Reviewer efficiency and auditability can improve significantly. Searchable inventories, structured notes, evidence attachments, and automated control follow-ups can reduce manual effort and strengthen traceability.

6. Downstream dependencies must be managed. Quickbase retirement will require more than moving data and UI components; the team must preserve or replace integrations relied on by other functions.

7. Recommended Immediate Leadership Focus

For the next AI Governance engineering planning meeting, focus on these three deliverables:

* GenAI Migration MVP Scope: Confirm which existing Quickbase features must be replicated in the first release and which enhancements can follow.
* Workflow and Lifecycle Design: Define the handling of approved-use-case changes, POC-to-production transitions, control acknowledgment, reviewer notes, and document evidence.
* Migration and Dependency Plan: Inventory current Quickbase backend workflows, reporting, and downstream integrations before planning retirement.

Overall recommendation: Deliver the unified GenAI intake experience first, but design it around the broader goal of a connected, auditable AI governance lifecycle—from initial use case registration through risk review, approval conditions, control implementation, and ongoing management.
