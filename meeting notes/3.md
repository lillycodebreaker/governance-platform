Meeting Summary — AI Governance Platform: Product Ownership, Priorities, Third-Party AI Exceptions & Production Readiness

Meeting focus: Clarify product versus engineering ownership, prioritize AI Governance capabilities, define third-party AI risk and exception workflows, and align on production readiness, data migration, user experience, and rollout planning.

Overall takeaway: The team emphasized end-to-end product ownership, stronger alignment between business and engineering, and a practical approach to delivering high-priority governance capabilities. The near-term focus appears to be the November production milestone, third-party exception management, and customer-facing AI assistance.

Note: This transcript contains substantial speech-recognition errors, especially around names, technical terms, and specific commitments. The summary distinguishes clear directions from items requiring confirmation.

1. Key Discussion Notes

A. Product Ownership and Engineering Responsibilities

The discussion emphasized the importance of clear ownership and accountability.

Business / Product Owner responsibilities

* Define business priorities, product vision, and roadmap.
* Gather and clarify business requirements.
* Own the desired user experience and business outcomes.
* Validate proposed capabilities with business stakeholders.
* Collaborate with engineering on priorities and delivery expectations.

Engineering / Technical Delivery responsibilities

* Own technical implementation and end-to-end engineering delivery.
* Evaluate architecture, security, scalability, and production readiness.
* Assess build-versus-buy alternatives and third-party integrations.
* Translate business requirements into technical designs and deliverables.
* Proactively identify technical gaps and recommend solutions.

Key message: Engineering should not simply wait for tickets or implement requirements without understanding the business objective. Product and engineering leaders should work together to define what needs to be built and how to deliver it.

B. Build vs. Buy and Internal Platform Ownership

The team discussed leveraging internal engineering capabilities rather than relying unnecessarily on external vendors.

* Internal ownership provides greater control over customization, architecture, integrations, and future expansion.
* Third-party solutions should be considered when there is a compelling technical or business reason.
* Begin unfamiliar capabilities with focused proof-of-concept work.
* Consider the full product lifecycle, including vendor relationships, operational support, and future enhancements.
* Use previous third-party integration experience to inform technical decisions.

Direction: Favor maintainable, extensible internal solutions where appropriate, while evaluating vendors based on specific requirements and trade-offs.

D. Third-Party AI Risk and Exception Management

A major discussion centered on how to govern third-party AI solutions without unnecessarily duplicating existing review processes.

Current considerations

* Third-party vendors may introduce AI capabilities into CVS systems or business processes.
* Existing procurement and third-party intake processes may already involve architecture, cloud security, privacy, or other reviews.
* AI Governance needs to understand the AI-specific risks, applicable controls, and existing approvals.
* The appropriate review process may differ depending on risk level, data sensitivity, and the nature of the integration.

Proposed platform capability

Introduce a third-party AI exception form within the new AI Governance platform to capture and track exceptions.

Potential requirements discussed include:

* Associate an exception with an AI use case or registration record.
* Capture relevant vendor or third-party engagement information.
* Record the exception and support visibility into its review status.
* Apply risk-based review and approval requirements.
* Support accountability through designated owners and reviewers.
* Eventually connect third-party engagement records to the broader AI Governance inventory.

Future opportunity: Integrate with existing third-party engagement or procurement systems so that relevant AI Governance workflows can be triggered automatically.

The discussion also suggested avoiding unnecessary reviews for lower-risk situations while ensuring appropriate oversight for higher-risk use cases.

E. Risk-Based Controls and Approvals

The team discussed aligning AI Governance processes with risk classification and control requirements.

Areas mentioned included:

* AI risk levels and assessment criteria.
* Privacy, compliance, and patient-safety considerations.
* Third-party vendor risk.
* Security and architecture reviews.
* Reviewer or accountable-owner approval requirements.
* Exception documentation and reporting.

Important distinction: The transcript suggests a desire to streamline low-risk activities and require stronger oversight for higher-risk cases. However, the precise risk thresholds, mandatory signoffs, and approval sequence were not clearly established.

F. Production Readiness and Data Migration

The discussion made clear that completing engineering development does not automatically mean the platform is ready for production.

Key readiness considerations:

* Engineering implementation and testing completion.
* Stable, scalable, and secure platform operation.
* Validation of registration and review workflows.
* Business confirmation of UI and functional requirements.
* Migration of existing AI inventory and model registry information.
* Handling legacy use cases and lifecycle updates.
* Communication, training, and operational support.

The team also discussed historical records accumulated over previous years. Decisions may be required on how much legacy information should be migrated and how existing model statuses should be represented.

Key message: Release planning should address both technical deployment and business adoption.

G. UI Review, User Feedback, and Communication

The team emphasized obtaining final business and user feedback before releasing the new platform.

* Review the customer-facing registration interface with appropriate business stakeholders.
* Align with Crystal, Sandy, and relevant governance representatives.
* Communicate changes to model owners and impacted users.
* Collect feedback during testing and review it before production.
* Prepare user guidance, documentation, tutorials, or training.
* Confirm business acceptance of the final user experience.

A roughly two-week time frame was mentioned near the end of the meeting, but the exact milestone and deployment commitment are unclear.

6. Key Takeaways for Leadership

1. Clarify accountability. The Product Owner should drive business requirements, priorities, and desired outcomes, while engineering takes ownership of technical design and delivery. Both functions need shared accountability for the completed product.

2. Prioritize business-critical capabilities. The third-party AI exception workflow emerged as a potentially urgent requirement. Its priority relative to the customer-facing AI Assistant needs explicit confirmation.

3. Streamline risk-based governance. The platform should build on existing third-party due diligence and risk assessments, avoiding unnecessary duplicate reviews while preserving appropriate controls and accountability.

4. Treat production readiness as an end-to-end responsibility. Technical completion, legacy-data migration, UI acceptance, user feedback, communication, and operational support all affect launch success.

5. Establish a clear release decision process. The team needs agreed go/no-go criteria, named approvers, documented outstanding issues, and a realistic deployment plan.

Recommended immediate leadership priorities

* Third-party exceptions: Align the governance requirements, minimum viable workflow, and delivery priority against AI Assistant work.
* Business readiness: Arrange the final UI review with Crystal and Sandy, confirm migration expectations, and establish the feedback and communication plan.

This would turn the discussion into a clear execution plan while keeping future automation enhancements on the roadmap.
