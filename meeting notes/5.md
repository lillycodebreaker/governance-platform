Meeting Summary — AI Governance: Build vs. Buy Evaluation, Vendor Capabilities & Engineering Organization Alignment

Meeting focus: Evaluate third-party AI risk assessment solutions against internal engineering capabilities, align on build-versus-buy criteria, and discuss the engineering organization restructuring, product strategy, and AI Governance team integration.

Overall takeaway: The meeting covered two major topics: evaluating Coverbase versus Onboard AI and the internal AI risk assessment POC, and welcoming the AI Governance engineering team into the broader Data Portal organization while clarifying product strategy, responsibilities, and future direction.

Note: Some product and organizational names are unclear in the transcription. Vendor names and team labels should be verified before distributing these notes formally.

1. Key Discussion Notes

A. Build vs. Buy — AI Risk Assessment Capabilities

The Third-Party Security (TPS) organization already uses Coverbase, integrated with ServiceNow, for vendor security assessments.

The team is evaluating whether existing Coverbase capabilities could also support AI Governance requirements, instead of building equivalent functionality internally.

The three alternatives discussed were:

Option	Main consideration
Coverbase	Existing CVS vendor relationship; potentially broader feature set and faster implementation
Onboard AI	External solution previously demonstrated for AI evidence review
Internal AI Governance POC	Greater customization, architectural control, and reuse across governance capabilities

Purpose of the evaluation: Confirm that investment in the internal POC is justified and establish a defensible recommendation for leadership.

The discussion did not indicate a decision to abandon or pause the internal POC.

B. Coverbase Capabilities Demonstrated

1. Zero-Touch Vendor Assessment

Automatically collects available vendor information and documentation without requiring direct vendor outreach. It supports preliminary risk screening to determine which vendors warrant a more detailed assessment.

Value: Reduce assessment workload and prioritize higher-risk vendors.

2. Automated Evidence Review

Analyzes submitted questionnaires, contracts, and supporting evidence against predefined controls.

Capabilities demonstrated included identifying missing or inconsistent answers, evaluating control compliance, generating issue summaries, and supporting analyst review.

Value: Reduce manual document assessment and improve risk-review consistency.

3. Continuous Vendor Monitoring

Monitors external information for changes in vendor risk, including public communications, data-use terms, regulatory developments, and AI-related concerns.

Value: Identify changes that could require reassessment after the initial vendor approval.

The team considered these capabilities useful references for future AI Governance modernization, even if they are not all included in the current POC.

C. Internal POC Scope

The internal engineering effort is currently focused on automated third-party AI evidence review.

The POC aims to:

* Process documents already submitted by vendors.
* Evaluate evidence against defined AI risk controls.
* Identify gaps, inconsistencies, and potential exceptions.
* Assist analysts with risk assessment.
* Generate assessment findings for downstream review.

The control definitions were described as being developed with the governance stakeholders, with integration into an internal agent already underway.

Current scope boundary: Zero-touch vendor research and continuous monitoring are not part of the immediate internal POC.

D. Assessment UX and Reporting Opportunities

The Coverbase demonstration provided several ideas for the internal roadmap.

* Compliance scoring and issue classification.
* Detailed lists of findings and unresolved controls.
* Evidence-level review and traceability.
* Ability to correct findings and rerun analysis.
* Drafting follow-up requests.
* Generating finalized assessment reports for Legal, HR, governance reviewers, and other SMEs.

However, the team also emphasized avoiding unnecessary tool switching.

Preferred direction: Explore exposing assessment capabilities through APIs and the existing AI Governance user interface rather than requiring reviewers to move between multiple applications.

A finalized assessment report was identified as a particularly useful potential enhancement.

E. Integration and Long-Term Architecture

The team discussed several architectural considerations:

* Coverbase currently integrates with ServiceNow for Third-Party Security.
* Archer integration is unclear and requires investigation.
* Relevant third-party risk processes are moving from Archer toward ServiceNow.
* The Governance platform may need to integrate with ServiceNow and other enterprise systems.
* The team wants access to assessment outputs, decision rationale, and supporting evidence through APIs where possible.
* ServiceNow may eventually provide some capabilities currently offered through specialized vendors.

Architectural implication: Avoid making long-term investments in integrations that could become redundant as enterprise systems are consolidated.

F. Build vs. Buy Evaluation Criteria

The team agreed that the decision should use multiple criteria rather than feature comparison alone.

Evaluation dimension	Key questions
Time to value	How quickly can internal development or vendor implementation deliver business value?
Total cost	What are license, implementation, maintenance, and support costs?
Functional coverage	Which required features are available now versus needing customization?
Integration	Can the solution integrate with ServiceNow, Archer, and the AI Governance platform?
Customization	Can AI risk controls and review logic be configured to CVS requirements?
API capabilities	Can capabilities be embedded within the existing UI?
Auditability	Can the platform expose evidence, decision rationale, and assessment history?
Vendor reliability	What have existing CVS teams experienced with the vendor?
Strategic fit	Does the solution align with the long-term enterprise technology roadmap?

The discussion emphasized that buying may deliver capabilities faster, while building can provide greater control and extensibility.

2. Engineering Organization Alignment

The second portion of the meeting addressed organizational changes and team introductions.

A. Organizational Restructuring

The FT team is joining the broader Data Portal engineering organization.

Leadership clarified:

* No immediate changes to existing work or delivery expectations are anticipated.
* Current product direction and working relationships should continue.
* Mid- and long-term organizational changes remain possible.
* Leadership will evaluate future structure after becoming more familiar with the team and its capabilities.
* Team members were encouraged to communicate directly with leadership about questions or concerns.

B. Product Strategy and Engineering Charter

The FT team will continue supporting research, experimentation, and requests that do not fit neatly into existing product roadmaps.

However, leadership emphasized that reusable engineering work should ideally become part of the broader product portfolio.

Key principle: Avoid creating isolated solutions that cannot be supported or reused by existing platforms.

The discussion also mentioned:

* Potential expansion of existing products.
* Continued development of the Data Portal.
* A new product offering under development.
* AI-enabled and agent-accessible product capabilities.
* A broader 2027 product roadmap.

C. AI-First vs. Traditional Interfaces

Leadership clarified that AI-enabled interfaces are not intended to eliminate traditional user interfaces.

* Existing traditional UI capabilities remain important.
* Some capabilities may be designed with CLI-first or agent-accessible interfaces.
* Human-facing interfaces should remain available for most offerings.
* Completely autonomous, AI-only workflows are not currently the general operating model.

Direction: Expand AI capabilities while preserving usability, oversight, and practical fallback options.

D. AI Governance Engineering Team Introduction

The AI Governance engineering group was introduced as a newly established, dedicated team within the broader engineering organization.

Its mission is to:

* Modernize AI Governance operations.
* Consolidate previously separate utilities.
* Build and expand the centralized AI Governance platform.
* Support Crystal’s governance organization.
* Improve automation, scalability, and user experience.

The platform was described as approaching its first full production launch.

The team includes engineering leadership and developers such as Dan and Vincent, with potential additional engineering resources.

7. Key Takeaways for Leadership

1. Validate the build-versus-buy decision. The internal POC remains the current approach, but the existing Coverbase relationship provides a meaningful alternative that should be evaluated.

2. Focus on actual governance requirements. Automated evidence review is the immediate need. Other vendor capabilities may inform future priorities without expanding the current scope unnecessarily.

3. Favor integrated experiences. Governance analysts should be able to access assessment outputs, evidence, and decision rationale through a consistent workflow rather than switching between multiple tools.

4. Consider long-term enterprise architecture. ServiceNow migration and potential tool consolidation are important factors in vendor selection and integration investment.

5. Preserve continuity through organizational changes. The FT team’s existing charter and delivery responsibilities remain largely unchanged in the short term.

6. Encourage reusable AI capabilities. AI Governance and Data Portal engineering should coordinate on common infrastructure, agents, APIs, and product integration patterns.

8. Recommended Immediate Leadership Focus

For the next AI Governance engineering discussion, I would concentrate on three deliverables:

* Build-versus-buy decision matrix: Compare Coverbase, Onboard AI, and the internal POC against agreed business and technical criteria.
* Internal POC readiness assessment: Document implemented capabilities, remaining gaps, dependencies, and expected delivery milestones.
* Target-state integration view: Show how evidence review, risk controls, ServiceNow, governance records, and future monitoring capabilities should interact.

Overall recommendation: Continue advancing the internal POC while completing a structured vendor evaluation. The goal is not necessarily to reproduce every vendor feature, but to determine which approach provides the strongest combination of speed, control, integration, cost, and long-term value for CVS AI Governance.
